Boston’s Windiest Year: What the Record Actually Shows

Wind speed at Logan Airport, 1936–2025

Data Story
Boston
Weather
Wind
Published

July 22, 2026

Data Story  ·  Boston, MA  ·  Wind

2025 was the windiest year on record at Boston Logan Airport, going back to when continuous wind observations began there in 1936. The average wind gust for the year hit 30.6 mph — well above the roughly 23 mph long-term norm — and Logan logged gusts of 60 mph or higher on five separate days, more than in any other year of the 89-year record.

A deliberately partial picture

This post is narrower in scope than the temperature posts on this site, and it’s worth saying why up front. Boston’s annual average high and low temperatures are compiled year-by-year in public, ready-to-use datasets going back to the 1890s. Wind is not treated the same way: the underlying daily and annual wind measurements exist in NOAA’s archives, but nobody has published the full year-by-year average wind speed series in a form that’s readily accessible without a direct bulk download from NOAA’s climate data systems.

What follows, then, is built entirely from specific, documented reference points — individual facts reported by NOAA/NCEI, the Iowa Environmental Mesonet, and the Boston Globe’s weather coverage — rather than a reconstructed year-by-year time series. Where a number below is a single documented data point rather than part of a continuous series, that’s noted explicitly. A fuller post, with an actual annual series, is a natural thing to come back to if a direct route to NOAA’s underlying station data opens up.

Gusts up, sustained wind more ambiguous

Gusts and sustained wind speed are genuinely different measurements, not just two ways of describing the same thing. Sustained wind speed is the average flow over a two-minute stretch; a gust is a brief, sharp spike that can last a few seconds to half a minute. Boston’s 2025 story is really a story about gusts specifically: through the first half of 2025, gusts were averaging nearly 32.6 mph, more than 2 mph above the previous record pace set in 2010, and Logan recorded gusts of 30 mph or higher on 165 days that year — roughly two and a half times the yearly average for days that gusty.

Sustained wind speed tells a more ambiguous story. Through the first half of 2025, sustained wind speed averaged 12.28 mph — the highest since 2000, and above the trailing 30-year average — but still below the full 89-year average for the station. Taken at face value, that means Logan’s sustained winds have historically run higher, on average, over the full 20th-century record than they have in the more recent 30-year normal period, even as 2025’s gusts were setting all-time records. That’s a genuinely interesting tension worth investigating further, but it isn’t one this post can fully resolve without the complete annual series in hand.

Why Boston is windy at all

Boston’s wind isn’t a fluke of any one year. Among major U.S. cities, Boston is the single windiest, with average wind speeds about 2 mph higher than second-place Chicago (the city that actually carries the “Windy City” nickname) and roughly twice the average wind speed of Phoenix, the calmest major U.S. city. The explanation researchers point to is location: Logan sits on the coast, close to the North Atlantic’s active storm track, which is also why a warming ocean and atmosphere can plausibly push the upper end of storm intensity higher without necessarily changing the day-to-day average by much — a distinction that may help explain why 2025 broke gust records so clearly while its sustained-wind story stayed more mixed.
