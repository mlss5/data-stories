Data Story  ·  NATO & OECD DAC  ·  Defense and Aid Spending 2014–2025

Every government faces a version of the same tradeoff: how much to spend on security at home versus stability abroad. NATO asks members to spend 2% of GDP on defense. The OECD asks donors to give 0.7% of GNI in foreign aid. Almost no country has ever met both targets simultaneously — and the gap is growing.

2023 Preliminary

1 — Absolute Spending: Defense vs. Aid

The United States dwarfs all other donors on defense — but also on aid, until 2025. Toggle USA on and off to see the distribution among other allies more clearly. Note how Poland's defense bar surges after 2022, while its aid bar barely registers.

2 — Who Meets the Targets?

The vertical line marks NATO's 2% of GDP defense target; the horizontal line marks the 0.7% of GNI ODA target. The top-right quadrant — meeting both commitments — has historically been almost empty. Drag the year slider above to watch countries migrate across quadrants over time.

Defense spending as % of GDP (NATO definition). ODA as % of GNI (OECD DAC definition, GNI approximated from IMF GDP estimates). Reference lines: 2% NATO target (vertical), 0.7% ODA target (horizontal). Finland joined NATO April 2023; Sweden March 2024.
